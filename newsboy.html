Simulation: The Newsboy Problem

The classic stochastic inventory challenge, simulated over 365 days.

Problem Statement

A vendor buys newspapers every morning for $1.00 each and sells them for $1.50. Any newspapers that are not sold by the end of the day lose all their value (they are thrown away). Daily customer demand is uncertain and randomly varies between 30 and 80 newspapers per day.

The Challenge: If the vendor decides to always order a fixed number of newspapers every morning, what will their total profit be at the end of a year (365 days)? What is the optimal order quantity to maximize their long-term profit?

Step-by-Step Guide (Excel Monte Carlo Method)

  1. Set your constants: In separate cells, define the Cost ($1.00), Price ($1.50), and Order Quantity (e.g., 50).
  2. Apply a random condition (Discrete step): For Day 1, generate the daily demand using the function =RANDBETWEEN(30, 80).
  3. Calculate Sales and Profit: Use =MIN(Demand, Order_Quantity) to figure out how many were actually sold. Then calculate the daily profit: (Sales * Price) - (Order_Quantity * Cost).
  4. Expand the simulation: Drag this row of formulas down (or use Ctrl+Enter) to simulate 365 continuous days.
  5. Multiple simulations: Sum up the profit for the whole year. Then, use an Excel "Data Table" referencing the Order Quantity cell to test how your annual profits change if you order 40, 50, 60, or 70 newspapers instead.
